Parallel Architecture

Walking through Chicago, I'm drawn to geometric alignments and unexpected symmetries in the built environment. These photographs capture moments where architectural elements create visual patterns—sometimes intentional, often accidental, always revealing something about how we organize space and movement in the city.

[Photo: Parallel lines of Trump Tower and Wrigley Building]

Vertical Parallels

Michigan Avenue Bridge

The Trump Tower and Wrigley Building create parallel vertical lines when viewed from the right angle on the bridge. Two different eras of Chicago architecture accidentally aligned in a single frame.

[Photo: L train tracks creating geometric grid]

Grid System

Loop District

The L tracks above create a geometric grid that mirrors the street pattern below. Infrastructure as accidentally beautiful urban geometry—function creating form.

[Photo: Shadows creating triangular patterns]

Shadow Triangles

Millennium Park

Late afternoon shadows from the surrounding buildings create precise triangular patterns on the park's lawn. Temporary geometry that changes with the seasons and time of day.

[Photo: Repeating window patterns across buildings]

Window Rhythms

LaSalle Street Canyon

The repetition of windows across multiple buildings creates a visual rhythm that's almost musical. Each building contributes to a larger pattern that emerges only when seen from street level.

[Photo: Curved building reflections in glass]

Curved Reflections

River North

A curved building's reflection in the glass facade of its neighbor creates distorted but perfectly geometric patterns. Architecture photographing itself.

[Photo: Staircase creating parallel diagonal lines]

Diagonal Parallels

Cultural Center

The grand staircase creates multiple parallel diagonal lines that seem to extend infinitely upward. Classical architecture's approach to creating visual movement through repetition.

[Photo: Street intersection creating perfect cross]

Urban Cross

State and Madison

The intersection of State and Madison, viewed from above, creates a perfect cross with the surrounding buildings framing each arm. The city's grid system as geometric art.

[Photo: Bridge supports creating rhythmic arches]

Rhythmic Arches

Lower Wacker Drive

The support columns under Lower Wacker create a series of arches that recede into the distance. Infrastructure as accidental cathedral—purely functional elements that happen to be beautiful.

[Photo: Escalator creating parallel moving lines]

Moving Parallels

Union Station

Escalators create parallel lines that are always in motion, but maintain their geometric relationship. Movement and stasis coexisting in the same frame.
