Hacker Fonts

The main font on each Y Combinator company's website at launch, 2005–2026. Often the founders didn't choose it: their framework did.

What changed

  • 2005–2011: most sites used fonts already on the visitor's computer, like Arial and Verdana. Reddit launched in Verdana, the font of Hacker News, and Airbnb in Arial.
  • 2012–2017: Helvetica Neue was the most common font each year. Stripe launched in it in 2011.
  • 2019–2022: the most common choice was no font at all, just the operating system's own, which is what Bootstrap and Tailwind use by default.
  • Inter went from 8% of sites in 2020 to 32% in 2025.
  • Geist, the Next.js default since late 2024, went from 0% in 2022 to 12% in 2026, and Inter fell for the first time, to 28%.
  • Google Fonts copied into the site's own files, which Next.js does automatically, went from 11% of sites in 2021 to 43% in 2026.
  • Monospace body text went from 0% of sites in 2023 to 4.7% in 2026.

Method

The companies, batches and websites come from YC's startup directory. For each company, the Internet Archive capture of its homepage closest to its batch's Demo Day was parsed, along with up to four of its stylesheets, for font-family rules. If a stylesheet wasn't archived at that address, the nearest archived copy within a year was used.

Not counted: batches whose Demo Day hasn't happened yet, captures from two or more years before Demo Day (usually a previous owner of the domain), and captures that never mention the company's name. They still appear in the lookup, marked. Spelling variants of the same font, such as "Ptsans" and "PT Sans", are counted as one.
